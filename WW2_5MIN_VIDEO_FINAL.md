Fact two.

Fact three.

Japanese diplomats were using the Soviet Union as a back channel — actively negotiating terms of surrender. The emperor himself was pushing for peace.

The single sticking point: they wanted to keep the emperor on the throne.

The firebombing of Tokyo in March 1945 killed one hundred thousand people in a single night. More than either atomic bomb.

Whether the bombs were necessary — historians still argue. But Japan wasn't fighting to the last man. They were already at the table.

### THE HOLOCAUST WASN'T JUST THE SS — IT WAS ORDINARY SOLDIERS

Fact four.

For decades, post-war Germany held onto a comfortable myth: the regular German army — the Wehrmacht — fought a clean war. The atrocities were carried out by fanatics. The SS. The extremists.

That myth collapsed in the 1990s.

A photographic exhibition — simply called the Wehrmacht Exhibition — toured Germany showing documented evidence of ordinary soldiers executing civilians, building ghettos, assisting in mass shootings across Eastern Europe.

The exhibitions caused riots. People walked in expecting to see history. They walked out looking at photographs of their grandfathers.

The Holocaust didn't just require monsters. It required millions of ordinary people to participate — or look the other way.

That's the most disturbing fact of all.

Fact five.

Japan and Russia never signed a peace treaty.

The dispute is over four small volcanic islands north of Japan — the Kuril Islands — seized by the Soviet Union in the final days of the war. Japan wants them back. Russia refuses.

The two countries trade. They hold diplomatic meetings. Their leaders shake hands.

But no piece of paper exists that says the war is over.

Eighty-five million people died. And they couldn't even agree on how to end it.
